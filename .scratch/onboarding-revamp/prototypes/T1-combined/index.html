Stride — T1 visual north-star · combined direction

One identity, three motion characters

Synthesis of two rounds of prototypes into a single combined direction, built as 3 variations that share the same fonts, palette, numbered-progress device, and core identity — but differ in how a step transitions, how the progress device is arranged, and how the plan reveal choreographs. Auth is a deliberately dark, mascot-minimized "chamber" in every variation (per R2: auth and onboarding cannot share one mounted component, so continuity here is visual-language-only, not literal continuity). Onboarding and plan-reveal share a light register and are the same mounted tree.

Shared DNA (identical across all 3)

  • Type: Manrope (400–800) for headlines/UI, JetBrains Mono for progress labels, mono ledgers and meta text.
  • Palette: ink #0d101b + lavender #b3a0ff as the universal accent; light paper/card surfaces for onboarding + plan-reveal; a dark #0c0e16 "chamber" for auth.
  • Numbered-progress device: a hollow, outline-only numeral (Manrope 800, -webkit-text-stroke) paired with a tick rail — from T1/coachs-notebook — reused in every variation, just re-arranged (side rail / top bar / centered dial).
  • Mascot: Stry's pixel avatar (T1/coachs-notebook) is the full persona presence in onboarding + plan-reveal; reduced to a 6px signal dot at auth (T1-batch2/darkroom) — same reduction in all 3 variations.
  • A11y (R1): focus → <h2 tabindex="-1"> per step; one polite live region for validation only; real role="progressbar" with true aria-valuenow; validate-before-advance; opacity-only under prefers-reduced-motion; ≤250ms, ≤24px translate everywhere.

Rail Drift calm · vertical drift

The most patient of the three. The numeral + tick rail sits as a persistent vertical column beside the question — coachs-notebook's exact side-rail arrangement. Steps rise up from below and settle (220ms, translateY 20px, cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1)), mirroring that source's up/down convention. The plan reveal doesn't pop all at once — its ledger rows cascade in sequentially (60ms stagger, vertical rise), the slowest and most deliberate unveiling of the three. This is the direction that reads closest to a quiet, considered coaching session.

Transition: directional vertical slide+fade Timing: 220ms / cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1) / 20px Progress layout: left vertical rail Reveal choreography: sequential vertical cascade

Route Swing brisk · horizontal + staggered

The most energetic of the three. The numeral + tick rail is bent into a horizontal bar across the top — wayfinding's route-line arrangement built from the same hollow-numeral device. Steps swing in from the right (180ms, translateX 24px, a snappier cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1)), and once the card lands, the answer options themselves stagger in individually (28ms/item) — a distinct transition style from the other two (staggered-element reveal, not a single block fade). The plan reveal keeps the horizontal logic: the macro bar sweeps left-to-right and the legend/ledger stagger in the same direction.

Transition: directional horizontal slide + staggered options Timing: 180ms / cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1) / 24px Progress layout: top horizontal bar Reveal choreography: left-to-right sweep + stagger

Dial Snap fast · pure crossfade

The fastest and simplest of the three — a deliberate contrast case. The numeral is bent into a centered circular dial whose ring fills as you progress, rather than a rail or bar. Steps don't slide in any direction at all: they crossfade only, no translate, at 150ms — right at R1's motion floor. The plan reveal follows suit: the whole card resolves in one fast fade, no cascade, no stagger — the direct opposite of Rail Drift's sequential reveal and Route Swing's staggered sweep. This is the direction for a user who wants to move through the flow quickly without motion getting in the way.

Transition: pure opacity crossfade, no translate Timing: 150ms / ease-out / 0px Progress layout: centered dial Reveal choreography: single fast crossfade
